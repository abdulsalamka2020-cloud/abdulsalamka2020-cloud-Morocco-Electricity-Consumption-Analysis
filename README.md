# Morocco Electricity Consumption and Environmental Factors Analysis
**AnalystLab Africa – Experience Lab Internship – Week 5 

## 1. What this project does
Analyses one year (1 Jan – 30 Dec 2017) of 10-minute electricity readings from three zones in Morocco, together with temperature, humidity, wind speed and diffuse-flow measurements, to answer:
patterns across zones · highest/lowest hours and days · monthly variation · association with weather · highest/lowest zone · how history can support planning.

## 4. Key assumptions and decisions
- **Date format** is month/day/year (`1/1/2017 0:00`) and is parsed with an explicit format.
- **Consumption unit:** treating each reading as kWh per 10 minutes and summing gives an implausibly large yearly total, so readings are **never summed into total energy**; the analysis uses averages (mean demand), which keeps comparisons valid. The unit should be confirmed with the data owner.
- **Diffuse-flow variables:** their values are ~0 at night and peak at midday, like solar radiation, which contradicts the "emission gases" dictionary description. They are used only as supplementary variables.
- **Outliers:** 1,191 high Zone 3 readings (all July–August evenings) were kept as genuine peak demand.
- Correlation is reported as association, **not causation**.

## 5. Headline findings
1. Evening peak at 20:00 (~2× the 06:00 low) in all zones.
2. Summer (June–August) is the high-demand season; combined demand peaks in July.
3. Zone 1 highest average; Zone 3 lowest average but most variable and most seasonal.
4. Sunday is the lowest day, mainly because of Zone 2.
5. Temperature moderately positively associated with consumption; humidity's link is mostly time-of-day; wind speed weak.
